About the PSQI Assessment

The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) is a scientifically validated questionnaire that assesses your sleep quality over the past month. It evaluates 7 key components of sleep:

  • Subjective Sleep Quality
  • Sleep Latency (time to fall asleep)
  • Sleep Duration
  • Sleep Efficiency
  • Sleep Disturbances
  • Use of Sleep Medications
  • Daytime Dysfunction

Scoring: Total scores range from 0-21. A score greater than 5 indicates poor sleep quality.

Evidence-Based Sleep Solutions

Sleep Hygiene

Maintain a consistent sleep schedule, even on weekends. Your circadian rhythm thrives on predictability. Create a relaxing bedtime routine 30-60 minutes before sleep.

Relaxation Techniques

Practice progressive muscle relaxation or the 4-7-8 breathing technique. These activate your parasympathetic nervous system and signal your body it's time to rest.

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I)

Challenge anxious thoughts about sleep. Use stimulus control: only use your bed for sleep. If you can't fall asleep within 20 minutes, get up and do a calming activity.

Digital Detox

Avoid screens 1-2 hours before bed. Blue light suppresses melatonin production. Use night mode or blue light filters if you must use devices.

Study Schedule Optimization

Avoid intense studying 2-3 hours before bed. Your brain needs time to wind down. Use distributed practice throughout the day instead of late-night cramming.

Environment Optimization

Keep your room cool (65-68°F), dark, and quiet. Consider blackout curtains, earplugs, or white noise machines. Your sleep environment significantly impacts quality.

Caffeine Management

Avoid caffeine 6-8 hours before bedtime. Caffeine has a half-life of 5-6 hours, meaning half of it is still in your system hours later.

Exercise Timing

Regular exercise improves sleep quality, but avoid vigorous exercise 3-4 hours before bed. Morning or afternoon exercise is ideal for better sleep.
